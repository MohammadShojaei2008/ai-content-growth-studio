Portfolio sample 06 · short-form app ads

Four hooks. Four very different audiences.

Original 54–58-word speculative UGC-style scripts. Each is designed for a 20–30-second first-person delivery, with an audience-specific friction point, prompt-to-result moment, community/discovery reference, and one CTA.

Important: The app in these concepts is hypothetical. Features and scenarios are invented for demonstration; these are not real-user testimonials, customer outcomes, published ads or paid projects. Before production, verify the real app’s functionality and replace fictional experiences with honest demonstrations or clearly labeled dramatizations.

AUDIENCE 1 · Students / exam overload · 55 words

Hook → problem → prompt / result → discovery → CTA

“I had three chapters to revise and exactly zero idea where to start. I opened this app, typed “make me a 20-minute plan for biology,” and it built a checklist I could actually follow. Then I found study setups other students had shared. If your notes are winning, try turning one topic into a plan.”

Fictional first-person performance script; not a verified customer testimonial.

AUDIENCE 4 · New app builders / explain ideas · 57 words

Hook → problem → prompt / result → discovery → CTA

“I kept trying to describe my side-project idea in a giant paragraph. Nobody got it. Then I typed “turn my messy notes into a simple app concept” into this app, and it built a clearer starting point. I checked community examples for approaches I hadn’t considered. If your pitch is still scattered, try one specific prompt first.”

Fictional first-person performance script; not a verified customer testimonial.

Creative rationale

The hooks aim to make viewers recognize their own problem in the first two seconds, before introducing “this app.” The center of each script is a concrete typed prompt and a described output, rather than abstract claims of AI magic. These are draft concepts requiring a real product brief, feature verification and creator voice review before use.
