08 / Startup founder video · Speculative sample · Not commissioned

The meeting ended. The work didn't start.

A 75–85-second founder talking-head launch concept, adapted for LinkedIn and X. Fictional founder, fictional product (“RelayNote”), and fictional scenario. This is writing practice, not an endorsement or actual launch.

Brief and angle

Audience: Busy small-team founders and operations leads.

Imagined product: RelayNote, a hypothetical tool that converts meeting decisions into a draft task list for human confirmation.

Founder voice: Direct, slightly self-critical, specific; no inflated metrics or marketing superlatives.

Core insight: Most teams don't need another summary — they need a named next action.

Three alternative opening hooks

1. “We kept leaving meetings with perfect notes and absolutely no idea who was doing what.”

2. “Our team didn't have a meeting-notes problem. We had a Monday-morning problem.”

3. “Here's the most expensive sentence in our weekly meeting: ‘I thought you were handling that.’”

Founder talking-head script · Approximately 80 seconds

0:00–0:15 / Friction

“Our team didn't have a meeting-notes problem. We had a Monday-morning problem.

Every Friday, we'd finish a meeting with a beautifully organized document. By Monday, half the decisions were stuck in someone's inbox.”

0:15–0:32 / Discovery

“I realized the missing piece wasn't a better summary. It was one very ordinary question: Who owns the next step?

So we started prototyping a tiny workflow around that question.”

0:32–0:55 / Show, don't overpromise

“Meet RelayNote, our fictional example of what that workflow could look like.

It takes meeting notes and suggests a list of actions, owners, and deadlines. But nothing gets assigned until a person reviews it. Because an AI guess isn't an agreement.”

0:55–1:16 / The actual payoff

“Here's the part that matters: when a meeting ends, everyone can see the decisions still waiting for an owner. No magic. Just fewer places for a task to disappear.

If you're building software for teams, try this: show the moment the user's day gets better, not just the feature that makes your demo look clever.”

1:16–1:22 / CTA

“What gets lost between your team's meetings and Monday?”

Companion LinkedIn post

We were good at taking meeting notes. We were bad at deciding what happened next.

That gap inspired this fictional product concept: RelayNote, an AI-assisted draft of meeting action items that a human must confirm before assignment.

One lesson in designing the story: don't sell a summary as accountability. Show exactly where the handoff gets clearer — and where human judgment still matters.

What's one step your team regularly loses after a meeting?

Companion X post

We thought our meetings needed better summaries. They actually needed a clear owner for the next action.

Fictional startup launch concept: draft action items with AI, confirm them with humans. A summary isn't accountability.

Visual and compliance notes

Visuals: Cut from a dense notes document to three unassigned tasks, then show an explicit “review before assigning” step.

Delivery: Leave a beat after “Who owns the next step?” and avoid reading in an ad voice.

Before using with a real brand: Replace the fictitious product, founder, and claims with verified specifics from a real brief; obtain permission for any product UI or logos. Do not claim reduced workload or performance improvements without supporting evidence.
